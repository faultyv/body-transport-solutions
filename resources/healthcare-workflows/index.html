The five handoffs

This is the decision graph behind a hospital-to-mortuary transport request. Each handoff has an owner, a useful record and a clear boundary.

Authorised instructionResponsible organisation confirms the job, locations and intended receiving party.
Release readinessReleasing facility confirms access, contact and documentation status.
Dispatch assessmentRoute, timing, vehicle and controlled refrigeration requirement are assessed.
Direct transferCollection is recorded and the refrigerated vehicle moves directly to receiving.
Receiving close-outAuthorised receiver signs handover; records and vehicle cleaning are closed out.

What each organisation needs to know

Hospital or clinic

Confirm the release contact, access requirements, documentation status and receiving facility. The transport provider needs a clear collection instruction.

Mortuary or funeral parlour

Confirm the receiving contact, handover requirements and whether the request is local, overflow, inter-provincial or a repatriation collection leg.

Insurer or assistance coordinator

Share the instructing organisation, route, timing, named contacts and any authority already obtained from the responsible parties.

Receiving facility

Keep the receiving contact ready, confirm access and sign the handover record so the transport job has a clear end point.

Municipal or public buyer

Use the buyer's current procurement and environmental-health requirements. This page explains transport scope; it does not replace the buyer's specification.

Repatriation coordinator

Separate the KZN ground-transport leg from airline, consular, permit and receiving responsibilities held by the authorised coordinator.

Route brief checklist

  1. Collection: exact facility, access point and releasing contact.
  2. Destination: exact receiving facility and handover contact.
  3. Timing: requested collection window and after-hours status.
  4. Instruction: responsible organisation and documentation status.
  5. Special conditions: site access, permits or declarations already identified by the responsible party.

Cold control and transport evidence

The dedicated compartment is pre-cooled, set and monitored to the controlled cold condition required for the transfer. The operating setting follows the assignment; it is not advertised as one fixed temperature. Vehicle cleaning, collection notes, receiving notes and chain-of-custody fields create the operational evidence that belongs to the transport job.

Compliance boundary: The responsible authorised parties remain accountable for release documentation, permits, declarations, identification, receiving arrangements and any family or funeral services. Review the compliance context and the South African Government's R363 Regulations on the Management of Human Remains for source material.

Frequently asked questions

Can a location alone start a route request?

It can start a conversation, but dispatch needs the exact collection and receiving facilities, contacts, requested timing, instructing organisation and documentation status.

What if the receiving facility changes?

Tell dispatch and the responsible authorised parties before movement where possible. The receiving contact and handover record should match the actual destination.

Is this a medical or funeral service?

No. It is operational transport guidance for professional organisations. Body Transport Solutions does not provide medical advice, storage, preparation or funeral arrangements.
