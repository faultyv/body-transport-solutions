Write the transport boundary first

A good procurement brief starts with the exact service: direct collection and delivery of human remains between authorised locations using a dedicated refrigerated vehicle. It should then state what the supplier does not provide—storage, preparation, embalming, funeral arrangement or family administration—so the RFQ is evaluated against the correct operational role.

For a buyer searching for a refrigerated transport service provider, body transport contractor, deceased transport quotation or mortuary transport supplier, the decision is easier when route, receiving and record requirements are explicit. The buyer's own procurement rules, current tender documents, municipal guidance and legal advice remain controlling.

RFQ scope: six questions to answer

1. What moves?

State that the requirement is direct human remains transport, not storage or funeral arrangement. Identify whether the route is local, inter-provincial or a repatriation collection leg.

2. Where?

List the exact collection facility, destination facility, access instructions, responsible contacts and any site-entry controls. A town name alone is not enough for a dispatch assessment.

3. When?

Specify requested collection windows, handover timing, after-hours escalation and how schedule changes are communicated. Avoid promising a response time before the route is assessed.

4. What evidence?

Ask for collection and receiving notes, route records, chain-of-custody fields and vehicle cleaning or disinfection records appropriate to the assignment.

5. Who authorises?

Identify the instructing organisation, release contact and receiving contact. Keep permits, declarations, identification and family or funeral arrangements with the responsible authorised parties.

6. How is it reviewed?

Define the quotation format, invoicing route, incident escalation, record retention and supplier review criteria required by the purchasing organisation.

Supplier evidence worth comparing

A procurement team can compare like with like without rewarding vague marketing claims.

  1. Vehicle suitability. Dedicated refrigerated compartment, secure platform, lift-up rear access and cleanable non-porous surfaces.
  2. Cold-control process. The compartment is pre-cooled, set and monitored to the controlled cold condition required for the assignment rather than sold as one fixed number.
  3. Handover discipline. The collection and receiving parties, route details and signed handover are recorded for each accepted job.
  4. Cleaning close-out. Cleaning and disinfection records are completed as part of the vehicle close-out process.
  5. Scope clarity. Longer routes and repatriation support are assessed against documentation, permits and receiving arrangements supplied by authorised parties.

Use public procurement sources as context, not proof

KZN Health publishes quotation and tender documents that show how public buyers describe orders, supplier obligations and ancillary services such as transportation. Those documents change over time and may cover a completely different commodity or institution, so a supplier must not copy them as a universal specification.

Frequently asked questions

Can you quote without an exact facility?

A broad route context can start the conversation, but dispatch needs the exact collection and receiving facilities, requested timing, responsible contacts and documentation status before the transport scope can be assessed.

Can a funeral parlour use this as an SLA checklist?

Yes, as a discussion framework. Agree the actual service levels, escalation route, records, pricing and responsibilities in the parties' signed agreement.

What is excluded from the transport scope?

Storage, preparation, embalming, funeral arrangement, family administration and permits or declarations that belong to the responsible authorised party are outside the transport-only role.
